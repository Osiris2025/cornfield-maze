2. WHAT THIS SUPERSEDES
===============================================================================

FSD §M28 (docs/reference/CORN-FIELD-MAZE-FSD.md:1040) reads: "Built from primitives and
procedural materials like everything else (§17) — no third-party model, so no licence
question."

That line was written to get the sphere-blob replaced, and M28 honoured it: `Husk.cs` builds
the creature from 60 runtime primitives in a `CreatePrimitive` loop, animated by a C# sine.

**M34 supersedes the "built from primitives" method.** The creature becomes a real mesh,
rigged in Blender and exported as FBX, imported into Unity as a GENERIC rig. Measured reason,
not a preference: the primitive version is 60 renderers (60 draw calls) and has no armature,
no skinned mesh and no clips — it cannot be retargeted, cannot take a hand-made pose, and
cannot be animated past what the sine already does.

House style is unchanged: **procedurally generated, not sourced.** No third-party model, so
still no licence question — but that is a consequence of doing it procedurally, not a licence
to download one. If ANY file is ever sourced, the licence is read out of API metadata (never
off a search result), `source-art/<topic>/PROVENANCE.md` is written with licence, credit line,
source URL, retrieval date and sha256, and the raw source is committed beside it.

**The name is NOT part of this milestone.** FSD §M28: "The Husk" fits a scarecrow made of corn
husks better than it fitted the old blob, so it stays unless Todd says otherwise. He called it
"that blobby thing" — the complaint was the shape, not the word. Keep `DisplayName = "the Husk"`.

4. WHAT "MUSTY AND DANK AND SCARY" MEANS IN PIXELS
===============================================================================

Smell is not renderable. The order is to translate it into the things a renderer actually
has: value, roughness, colour, geometry damage, and motion. A scarecrow that smells musty
reads as one that has stood in a wet field through many nights — so it is STAINED, MOULDY,
SAGGING and ASYMMETRIC, and it moves like something heavy that is coming apart.

Aim for these reads. They are the difference between "a scarecrow prop" and "a thing that has
been rotting out there and is now walking":

  - **Burlap, not leather.** The head and any sacking is coarse woven cloth: a visible
    over-under weave, individual threads, slubs, and holes where threads have parted. Weave
    scale must be readable in the HEAD CLOSE-UP, or the head is a brown box again.
  - **Mildew and mould.** Mottled, desaturated green-grey-black patches that follow gravity
    and damp: in creases, under the arms, in the crotch of the coat, along the bottom. Never
    a uniform tint — mould grows in blotches with soft irregular edges.
  - **A tide line on the hem.** The bottom of the coat is WET and it shows a soft, irregular
    water line: the hem band is measurably darker and more saturated than the shoulder band
    (§8.5). This is the single strongest "dank" cue and it is measurable, so measure it.
  - **Rot and repair.** Split seams with straw pushing out, frayed edges, a patch sewn on
    with coarse stitches, a rusted nail or wire binding at the cross joints, dark bruised
    staining where the timber meets the cloth.
  - **Moss at the joints.** Where wood stays wet — the base of the post, the crossbar
    shoulders — a low creeping moss/lichen. Small, dark, and only where water would sit.
  - **Straw that is not golden.** Damp straw is grey-brown and clumped. It hangs and droops;
    it does not stick out like a fresh decoration.
  - **Timber that has been outside.** Silvered, checked, split ends, grain raised. The post
    should look heavier than the cloth — it is what holds the thing up.
  - **Asymmetry, everywhere.** One shoulder higher. One arm longer. The head cocked. One
    leg's sacking bound differently from the other. Symmetry reads as manufactured; this
    thing was assembled by a farmer out of what was lying around.

## 4.1 THE TRAP — DAMP IS NOT SHINY

**Todd's standing ruling on this exact material family (M32c, verbatim): "the sheen AT ALL is
a bug, maybe there should be a sheen in puddles, but not over all."** Damp cloth and damp
straw are ROUGH. Wetness darkens a surface and keeps it rough; it does not make it glossy.
The only thing in this game that gets any specular at all is standing water, and on 2026-09-25
he accepted even that as a plain dark wet patch with NO glint.

So for this creature, dampness is expressed as **DARKER + ROUGHER**, never shinier. Concretely:

  - Roughness on the wet hem band and the mouldy regions must be **≥** the roughness of the
    dry upper regions. If a "dank" pass produces a lower roughness number anywhere on the
    cloth, it is wrong and it is the exact bug he already called out.
  - No environment reflection on cloth. No gloss map carrying a low smoothness on the coat.
  - If you want a wet glint anywhere, the only defensible spot is a standing drip or a wet
    patch of ground, and §4.1's answer there is still "a dark wet patch", not a highlight.
  - This creature is a menace at night and the moon light is capped near 0.52 intensity
    (`DuskSky.cs:380`). A highlight you buy by raising the moon relights the whole maze, which
    is ruled out. The dark is not a bug to fix — it is the scene.

## 4.2 MOTION IS HALF THE SMELL

A shambling mound is not a walk cycle with a limp pasted on. The read is: too heavy, coming
apart, and dragging itself forward because it wants to. Secondary motion is what sells it —
the straw and the cloth must arrive a beat after the body does.

5. GEOMETRY — what it is made of
===============================================================================

Keep the M28 identity that already works (the silhouette test passed on the T, the tilted
sack head, the stitched mouth, the hollow sockets with the faint night glow) and add what is
missing: legs, weight, and rot.

  - **Legs, and they are not a matched pair.** Two legs with thigh / shin / foot. One is
    shorter or bound differently so the gait is uneven by construction. Feet are heavy and
    uneven — one boot, one sacking-wrapped stump, or one bare splintered foot. A foot that
    plants flat and clean reads as a person.
  - **The cross is the frame, not a prop beside it.** With legs, the weathered timber becomes
    the spine and hips: the post runs up through the body and the crossbar is the shoulders,
    so the bar pushes the coat out at the top and the post shows below the torn hem. M28
    already had the post showing under the hem — keep that, it is the "scarecrow" tell.
  - **The head** stays a sack on a wrong tilt, with the stitched mouth and the hollow sockets
    holding the faint glow at night. It must LOLL as it walks (§6).
  - **The coat** is tattered and stained, sleeves ending in straw rather than hands.
  - **Height.** M28 measured 2.31 m against the cookie's 1.80 m and the corn's 2.90–3.20 m,
    so it breaks the lane line and not the canopy. Keep it in that band. State the new
    measured height — do not assume the old one still holds once it has legs and a posture.

## 5.1 Poly and draw-call budget — this should be a WIN, not a cost

The creature today is 60 renderers = 60 draw calls. A single skinned mesh is 1–2. So:

  - Target **≤ 12,000 triangles** for the base mesh. Say the actual number.
  - **One skinned mesh, one or two materials** — an opaque body material and, if needed, a
    cutout material for straw cards. Not one material per part.
  - Straw/leaf cards are alpha-cutout. **FBX carries no blend mode** — set Cutout on import in
    Unity, or the cards render as solid slabs. This is a known trap in this project.
  - The acceptance is the measured frame time against the M28 baseline (§8.6): avg 2.35 ms,
    p95 3.33 ms, worst 4.34 ms on the Mac build. The phone 60 fps floor is the real target and
    no device is attached, so report the Mac number and say plainly that the phone is unmeasured.

6. RIG AND CLIPS — "fully rigged" means the clip set, not the skeleton
===============================================================================

Load the `blender-headless-rig-and-export` skill before writing any rig code. Its §1 records
the probe results for THIS host (Blender 5.0.0 Beta, build 2025-11-02): armature, automatic
weights, pose mode, `bake_anim` and Rigify-enable are all confirmed present, numpy is 1.26.4,
and a full build→skin→pose→bake→export→re-import round trip returns armature 1, bones 4,
meshes 2, action 1, **0 unweighted of 16**. Re-probe with `scripts/probe_rig_capability.py`
if the build changes.

## 6.1 Rig the shape it IS, not the shape a human is
An autorig is a T-posed-biped tool. **Do not put a Rigify humanoid metarig on this.** Hinge
where the object actually hinges, and let the loose material sway on light weights:
root → hips → two legs (thigh/shin/foot each) → spine ×2 → shoulder bar → two arms (upper/fore)
→ neck → head → jaw, plus secondary bones for straw cuffs, sleeve tips and the coat hem.
Keep it **≤ 32 bones**. Rigid parts hinge rigidly; only cloth and straw deform softly.

## 6.2 Skin, then COUNT what was weighted
`parent_set(type="ARMATURE_AUTO")`, then print per-group vertex counts and the count of
vertices with NO group. **`0 unweighted of N` is the number that means the skin arrived.**
An unweighted region deforms as a detached island and the frame looks like a broken model.
Report the group count both before AND after the round trip — the exporter drops zero-weight
groups, so a part can come back with fewer groups and still be correct. It is only *known*
correct because the unweighted count was checked on the re-imported mesh.

## 6.3 Clips — the deliverable. Name them.
In-place clips, **no baked root motion** — the controller keeps driving position and root
motion would fight it. Import at 30 fps.

| clip                 | what it is                                                        |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `idle_teeter`        | standing, swaying on the post, head lolling. It should look like a thing that is NOT supposed to be moving. |
| `shamble_advance`    | THE WALK — the tell. See §6.4.                                    |
| `reach_lunge`        | both arms up, torso pitching forward, the mouth opening.           |
| `stagger_hit`        | the hit reaction. **Must be 1.10 s** — it is the M23 contract.     |
| `collapse_scatter`   | the frame folding and the husk scattering. It re-forms at 8 s.     |

Optional but cheap and worth it: `alert_snap` — the head snapping toward the player. The
moment it notices you is a scare the walk cannot deliver.

## 6.4 The gait is the whole character, and it is already pinned to a beat
The existing tell is measured: instantaneous speed 0.00→3.64 m/s, one surge every **0.72 s**,
peak 1.55× base speed. Do not replace that with a round 1.0 s stride — author the walk to the
0.72 s beat so the footfalls land ON the surges. The code's sine and the clip's steps have to
agree or the thing skates.

Make it a SHAMBLE, and measure these three things:

  1. **Asymmetry.** Left-step duration vs right-step duration, reported as a ratio. Target
     ≥ 1.25× so the limp is unmistakable. A 1.0 ratio is a walk, not a shamble.
  2. **Foot slide.** The planted foot's horizontal speed during stance at `MoveSpeed` 2.35 m/s.
     Target ≤ 0.15 m/s. Foot slide is the single most common way a hand-authored walk looks
     cheap, and it is measurable, so measure it.
  3. **Drag.** The bad foot should scuff — a lower foot lift on one side and a visible ground
     contact through the stance. Report the two sides' foot-lift heights.

Secondary motion, which is what actually sells "shambling mound": the head lags and snaps;
the straw cuffs and the coat hem trail the body by a beat. Author the delay in the secondary
bones and say what the delay is in frames.
