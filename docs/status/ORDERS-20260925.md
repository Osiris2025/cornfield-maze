2. THE DEFECT — Todd, 2026-09-24, looking at our own sky-t40.png
=============================================================================
  "that picture of the 'moon' doesnt show a halloween moon, it shows wha appears to me to be
   a blob."

He is right, and the cause is exact: `DuskSky.SkyAtlas` (DuskSky.cs:496-507) paints the moon as a
perlin patch — `maria = 0.72 + 0.28 * PerlinNoise(dx * 2.4, dy * 2.4)` — with a 10 % soft limb. At
6.6-6.8 degrees of sky that is a pale disc with mottling: no maria, no craters, no limb structure,
no glow. It was never going to read as a moon. The fix is not tuning that noise — it is a photograph.

3. THE ASSET — already sourced, built and committed by Ernie. Do not re-source it.
=============================================================================
* **Texture:** `Assets/Resources/Sky/T_Moon_Full.png` (1024x1024 RGBA), built by
  `scripts/moon_build.py` (design-time, run under Blender) from a public-domain NASA SVS
  full-phase frame. Provenance, licence, credit line and sha256: `source-art/moon/PROVENANCE.md`.
  Credit line: **NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio.** Public domain — no obligation attaches.
  (The obvious Wikimedia full moon was rejected deliberately: CC BY-SA 3.0, attribution AND
  share-alike. Do not replace this with a web photo whose licence has not been checked.)
* **What the texture contains:** RGB = the disc's real albedo (maria, Tycho's rays, crater
  speckle); A = the disc mask, softened over the outer 2 %; and in the margin around the disc, a
  baked amber glow (atmospheric scatter — the bloom in every Halloween reference).
* **GEOMETRY THE WIRING MUST HONOUR:** the disc's diameter is **0.62 of the texture width**. So a
  quad that should subtend angle A must be sized **A / 0.62** — the moon quad becomes 1.613x its
  old size, and only the inner 62 % of it is the moon. Too small and the moon shrinks; too large
  and the halo eats the sky. `scripts/moon_build.py` prints both numbers on every run. If you
  change DISC_FRACTION, the wiring changes with it — see the provenance file.
* `artifacts/reference/moon-built.png` is the built sprite on a flat background. It is a flat
  preview: it does NOT alpha-composite, so the glow's outer edge looks like a hard rim there. In
  the sky it composites smoothly. Judge this pass on a sky frame, never on that preview.

4. THE WORK
=============================================================================
**Squall owns this pass.** Make the moon in the sky be that photograph. Requirements:
1. The moon reads as a moon at 6.6-6.8 degrees: maria and crater detail visible, a limb that is
   round, and a warm halo bleeding into the sky around it.
2. **Keep §25.5's beats exactly as they are:** the dusk ramp at t=0, the rise 0-40 s, the
   deliberately oversized angular size, the oblate squash near the horizon, and cloud occultation
   computed from the cloud and moon directions. This is a look change, not a redesign.
3. **Warm it like the references.** The quad's colour multiplies the albedo, so the disc takes the
   tint you pass it: a harvest-amber moon against a torn moonlit sky is the target. Check both
   t=0 (low, warm, big) and t=40 s (high, night).
4. Keep the single-draw-call discipline if you can — the atlas has three regions and the moon is
   region 0 — but if a separate material for the moon is the honest way, take it and say so.
5. If the night sky (§23.5, `NightSky.cs`) also shows a moon, it must be the same moon. If it does
   not, say so in the report, so it is a known fact rather than an assumption.
