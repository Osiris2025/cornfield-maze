2. M29 — THE GROUND (Todd, 2026-09-25)
=============================================================================
Todd supplied six photographic texture sets and asked for realistic derived ground with a
SUPERIMPOSED path, blended. It is built and committed as `7b85f5f`. Your job is to put it in the maze.

Read `source-art/ground/PROVENANCE.md` first — it carries the licence record (six Poly Haven sets,
CC0), the source checksums, the measured tileability, and the known synthetic tells. That file is the
single source of truth for what these textures are; do not re-derive or re-grade them.

What exists, in `Assets/Resources/Ground/`, all 1024x1024:
  T_Ground_Field.png / _N.png / _R.png      leaf litter + mud over damp soil — the field floor
  T_Ground_Lane.png  / _N.png / _R.png      worn gravel + stones + edge grass — the walked lane
  T_Ground_LaneEdge.png                     tileable fbm; the ragged boundary mask
Reference: `artifacts/reference/ground-preview.png` (what ships) and `-raw.png` (ungraded).

1. **Replace the noise.** `Materials.cs` builds `Gravel` (128 px noise), `FieldGrass` (128 px) and
   `PathGrass` (96 px); `MazeWorldBuilder.cs` consumes them. At 128 px a noise tile can only read as
   synthetic, which is why the floor looks flat. These replace them. Keep the old code path only if
   something still needs it, and say what.
2. **The lane is blended by TRANSPARENCY, not layered.** Todd, 2026-09-25: "the path way should not be
   layered, it should be blended via transparancy. the path is currenly bubbly and janky." His read was
   right, and it was a method error: a strip composited with its half-width pulsing on a low-frequency
   mask produces large smooth blobs that read as a separate layer lying on the field. What ships:
   - the lane fades out across its width over a WIDE band, and that fade is eaten into at a FINE scale,
     so the margin dissolves into the field instead of being drawn on it;
   - `Assets/Resources/Ground/T_Ground_LaneAlpha.png` is that fade as a strip -- U runs along the lane,
     V across it, solid in the middle and gone at both edges, fine grain breaking the ramp. Map the lane
     mesh's UVs so V crosses the lane and drive the lane material's alpha from it (Alpha Blend, NOT
     Opaque or Cutout). If URP Lit cannot carry a separate alpha map, multiply it in a small in-house
     Shader Graph or use vertex alpha -- but the **result is non-negotiable**: no hard line, no blobs,
     no visible edge where the layer stops.
   - A lane does NOT pulse: constant half-width. The low-frequency wobble is what made it bubbly.
   - `T_Ground_LaneEdge.png` remains available as a coarse mask if you need one; the fine grain in the
     alpha strip is what does the dissolving.
3. **Scale in metres.** State the metres-per-tile you settled on and why it matches the source: a 1K
   pass over roughly 2 m of ground is the honest reading. Show it against the 4 m cell — a wrong tiling
   is the fastest way to make the best texture look like wallpaper.
4. **Import settings, exactly:** albedo -> sRGB, Wrap Repeat. Normal -> Texture Type *Normal map*,
   Non-Color, Repeat, and **do not flip the green channel** (the sets are `nor_gl`, OpenGL convention,
   which is Unity's). `_R` roughness and `LaneEdge` -> Non-Color, Repeat, sRGB OFF. State in the report
   whether you drove smoothness from `_R` (and how you packed it) or a constant, and why.
5. **Phone weight.** Six 1024² maps is heavier than a phone wants. Measure the texture memory you added
   and report it; if it is over budget for the 60 fps floor (FSD §25.2), produce 512² variants, say so,
   and keep the full-res set for the desktop build. Do not silently downscale.
6. **Do not touch:** the corn, the HUD, the maze layout (the six block seeds and the layout hashes are
   in the FSD and must not move), M25b's moon, M26's threat term, or the eat sequence.
7. **For Todd's decision, shoot the variants.** The ground exists in three built variants -- `supplied`
   (his 1K leaf litter), `dry` (2K cornfield, smooth compacted lane) and `mixed` (cornfield field with a
   grainy dirt-and-stone lane). They write the same seven filenames, so switching is a rebuild and not a
   code change:
       GROUND_SHIP=dry /Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -noaudio --python scripts/ground_build.py
   Once M29 is green on the shipping variant, re-shoot the SAME three frames for the other two and put
   them in the report as a comparison sheet -- first person, standing on a lane, at night. Todd picks
   from frames, not from thumbnails. Do NOT pick for him and do not ship a variant he has not seen at
   night.
8. Do not edit the FSD or the locked docs. The license record lives in
   `source-art/ground/PROVENANCE.md`; if the FSD needs a §17 revision to describe the new ground,
   report the text and Ernie applies it.

Evidence (Lantern): a fresh Mac build; `artifacts/review/world/m29-ground-lane.png` (night, ground
level, looking down a lane — the field on both sides, the lane underfoot, the margin ragged),

4. M28 — THE CHASER BECOMES A SCARECROW
=============================================================================
Todd, 2026-09-25: "'Husk' is silly looking.. we need scary, hungry scarecrow chasing gingy." FSD
§25.8. Owner **Dough**, with Furrow only on the hit volume and the walk timing.

The diagnosis is not a matter of taste. `Husk.cs:129-175` builds the creature from **spheres**: a
blue-grey body sphere at 1.15 scale, a belly, a mouth sphere with two tooth cubes, **googly white eye
spheres with black pupils**, crumb balls and feet. It is a cartoon mascot, and in the frame it reads as
a thin dark pole.

Build a **scarecrow** out of the same procedural register (§17) — primitives and procedural materials,
no third-party model, so no licence question:
1. A rough cross of weathered timber: one upright, one crossbar, both visible where the coat does not
   cover them.
2. A burlap sack head, slightly loose, with a **stitched mouth** (a seam of dark stitches, not a grin)
   and **hollow sockets** — no eyeballs. At night the sockets hold a faint glow; that is the only light
   on it.
3. A body of dry husks and straw bursting out of a **tattered coat**: sleeves that end in straw, not
   hands; straw out of the neck and cuffs; the coat hem torn.
4. **Taller than the cookie — about 2.2 m** — so it breaks the corn line as a silhouette.
5. **The head tilts wrong**, and it stays tilted: this is a scarecrow that has decided to move, and the
   wrongness is the scare.
6. The walk is a **lurch**, not a glide: the tell of §25.4's timing. Keep its speed and catch rules
   exactly as they are (2.35 -> 3.40 by L20, catch 0.62); this is a look change, not a balance change.
7. The **hit volume** must still be honest: M23's cob has to hit it, and the flow law (§25.4: no threat
   holds the player still more than 1.5 s) is unchanged. If the new silhouette is wider than the old
   capsule, say so and give the measurement.
8. **The name stays "the Husk."** It fits a scarecrow made of corn husks better than it fitted the blob.
   Do not rename anything in this pass.

Evidence (Lantern): `artifacts/review/world/m28-scarecrow-lane.png` (moonlit, at a lane's end),
`m28-scarecrow-close.png` (the head: sockets, stitched mouth, tilted), and `m28-scarecrow-silhouette.png`
— **the silhouette test is the acceptance test**: at 12 m at night, with the material collapsed to a
flat black shape, the frame must still read as a scarecrow. Say plainly whether it does;
`artifacts/m28-scarecrow-report.txt` carries the measured height, the hit volume against M23's cob
strike, and the lurch timing against its speed.
