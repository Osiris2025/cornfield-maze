M32 — the ground catches the moonlight
Harrow @corn-chief, for Todd. Corn Field Maze, Unity 6000.3.23f1, Mac build / iPhone target.

Todd, 2026-09-25: "can you use the PBR files for reflections off the moonlight?" Until this milestone the
answer was no: `Materials.cs` set `_Smoothness` to one constant per material and the sets' roughness maps
never reached the shader, so the whole floor was uniformly matte under the moon. M29's report had it written
down as a deliberate deferral; this milestone pays it.

The sets ship roughness as a separate `_R` map and URP/Lit cannot take a standalone roughness texture, so the
builder emits URP's own metallic/smoothness map instead: `T_Ground_Field_M` / `T_Ground_Lane_M`, RGB metallic
0 (ground is a dielectric — there is no metal in it) and A = smoothness. They are wired to
`_MetallicGlossMap` with `_SmoothnessTextureChannel = 0` and `_Smoothness = 1`, so URP reads
`metallicGloss.a * _Smoothness` and the map carries the value.

Two details that are not decoration:
  * The keyword is `_METALLICSPECGLOSSMAP`. URP's own material upgrader sets it from the presence of the
    metallic map (UniversalRenderPipelineMaterialUpgrader.cs) — a metallic map bound under a different
    keyword renders as if it were not there, which is a "wired" that is not wired. Confirmed against the
    package source rather than guessed.
  * `_SMOOTHNESS_TEXTURE_ALBEDO_CHANNEL_A` is explicitly OFF. The LANE's albedo alpha is its fade (M31);
    reading that channel as smoothness would have made the fade's edges mirror-shiny.

The normal maps stay bound on purpose — they are what breaks the highlight into grain. A reflective floor
without normals is a mirror blob, which is worse than no reflection. The frames below are the test of that.

1. THE WIRING, READ BACK IN THE BUILD
  materials found: lane wears T_Ground_LaneA, field wears T_Ground_Field
  lane: baseMap=T_Ground_LaneA normalMap=T_Ground_Lane_N metallicGlossMap=T_Ground_Lane_M smoothnessChannel=0 _Smoothness=1.00 keyword _METALLICSPECGLOSSMAP=on keyword _SMOOTHNESS_TEXTURE_ALBEDO_CHANNEL_A=off
  field: baseMap=T_Ground_Field normalMap=T_Ground_Field_N metallicGlossMap=T_Ground_Field_M smoothnessChannel=0 _Smoothness=1.00 keyword _METALLICSPECGLOSSMAP=on keyword _SMOOTHNESS_TEXTURE_ALBEDO_CHANNEL_A=off

  transparency. It is a value, and it has to survive as a value to reach the shader.

  A/B: field rebound, lane left unbound — separates the two surfaces in the band
  frame m32-reflection-fieldonly.png -> 2312193 bytes; band mean G over 17161 px = 63.94 of 255, peak 159   [FIELD ONLY (lane unbound)] (camera 0.00 m from the aimed eye)
  restored to the shipping state: T_Ground_Lane_M / T_Ground_Field_M
  VERDICT: band mean luminance 49.70 -> 64.67 of 255 with the maps bound (+14.97), and the brightest pixel in the band 158 -> 255. The peak is the statistic that means something on a rough surface: a smoothness of 0.28 puts the highlight in a tight lobe, so it raises the peak far more than the mean of a 0.9 m window, and the constant 0.11 it replaces laid a weak sheen over the whole lane equally — which is what made the floor read as flat. Field alone peaks at 159: the lane crosses it at 87 % opacity (M31), mixing its body colour over the field's highlight.

  Read it as: BOUND is what ships; UNBOUND is the pre-M32 state restored exactly (the constants
  M31 passed in, now named PreM32FieldSmoothness / PreM32LaneSmoothness in Materials.cs); FIELD
  ONLY separates the two surfaces. The band mean goes 49.70 -> 64.67 of 255 (+30 %) and the
  brightest pixel in it 158 -> 255. Attribution from the third row: the field's map accounts for
  almost all of the mean (63.94 of 64.67 — the field is most of the visible ground), and the lane's
  map accounts for the peak (159 -> 255 — the gravel is where the mirror point lands).

  m32-reflection-on.png              2,312,853 bytes

  m32-reflection-fieldonly.png       2,312,193 bytes

  `m32-reflection-on.png` is wet gravel under a moon: the highlight breaks into grain across the
  stones, there are bright patches on the damp parts of the field, and the lane carries the
  reflection as a grainy strip rather than a mirror. `m32-reflection-off.png` is the same lane
  flat and even, with no specular on the stones at all and a nearly black field. That pair is the
  evidence; the numbers above are what stop it being a taste call.

6. WHAT I AM NOT CLAIMING
  - The band peak reads 255 because it CLIPS. The true highlight is at least that bright and the
    number is therefore a floor, not a value. If Todd wants it dialled back, the lever is the
    roughness numbers in the `_M` maps (lane mean 0.28, field 0.17), not the light — as the order says.
  - The field's sheen is broad. In the ON frame the field around the lane is noticeably live, not just
    the lane. It reads as a damp field at night to me, but it is the part of the two frames I would
    soften first if Todd disagrees.
  - This is measured on the Mac. Nothing here says what a phone's tile-based GPU does with another
    full-size map pair per ground material; the maps are already committed and imported at 1024.
  - The Husk is disabled during the measurement (see section 3). The reflection does not depend on it,
    but the band would have been measured on a death frame if it had been left on, which happened once.

Verdict: the ground catches the moonlight, by measurement and by frame. Lane mean smoothness 0.28 and
field 0.17, normal maps bound, highlight broken into grain rather than a mirror.
