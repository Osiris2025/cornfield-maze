FIXED THIS PASS — THE CAMERA NOW STANDS ON THE LANE

Passes 1 and 2 both framed corn, because the harness tried to work out where the lane was. It extrapolated
the standing spot from the decal's own axis (pass 1), then raycast the world looking for the lane's material
(pass 2). Both put the player in the corn. The maze already knows where the lane is, so this pass asks it:
start at the puddle's own cell, step in each of the four directions while the next cell is a path cell, and
take the lane cell whose distance is closest to the mirror distance. The result, from the built app:

  stand: (36.00, 0.08, 44.00) — 4.00 m from the puddle's centre, on maze cell (9,11), 1 step(s) along the
  lane from the puddle's cell (9,10). The distance is 3.12 m because that is where a 28 deg moon reflects
  onto the water at an eye height of 1.655 m, and the cell is a path cell, so the stand is on the lane by
  construction, not by geometry

The frame confirms it: artifacts/review/world/m32b-puddle-moon.png is now shot down a gravel lane with corn
on both sides, instead of the inside of a corn block. One cell is 4.00 m, so the nearest lane cell to the
mirror distance is 4.00 m away rather than 3.12 m; the reflection therefore lands 0.88 m short of the
puddle's centre, which is still inside a decal 3.4 m long.

STILL OPEN — THE MOON IS NOT CAUGHT IN THE WATER

The acceptance is a night frame with the moon caught in a puddle. The second half of that is in frame: the
lane around the mirror point reads matte, and with the reflective maps unbound the band's mean G drops from
41.69 to 21.50 of 255 with the peak falling from 220 to 127 — that is the ground's own behaviour, and it is
the matte behaviour Todd asked for. The first half is not: there is no moon-shaped highlight in the water.
The only bright thing in the frame is the sky through a gap in the corn.

What I would test next, in this order:
  1. sample the PUDDLE'S OWN PIXELS against the LANE'S PIXELS in the same frame, at the same distance from
     the camera. If the water is not measurably darker or brighter than the lane beside it, the decal is not
     reading at all and the problem is the decal (its alpha coverage, its lift height of 6 mm, its blend
     mode), not the light.
  2. if the decal reads, the glint is a material/lighting question: MoonLight intensity at night is
     0.30-0.52 with shadows off, the water's normal is a deliberately flat plane, and smoothness reaches the
     shader as 0.875 x _Smoothness 1.0. A flat 0.875 surface under a 0.30-0.52 directional light at 28 deg
     may simply have no specular worth seeing — in which case the honest answer is that the sheen has to come
     from the water's own response, and that is a taste call for Todd, not a bug to keep chasing.
