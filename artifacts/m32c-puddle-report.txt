Six captures in ONE frozen camera pose. The water's pixels and the lane's pixels are no longer guessed from

THE REFLECTION PROBE, ON THE WATER'S OWN PIXELS: -0.01 OF 255

    probe ON water mean 92.51, probe OFF 92.52; mean |change| across the water 0.14 of 255;
    water pixels brighter than 140: 3,882 with the probe, 3,889 without.

Two passes now agree, and this time over the water's own pixels rather than a geometry guess: **the
reflection probe contributes nothing to the water.** Pass 1 said so from two different cameras (void); pass 2
said so from the wrong pixels (void); this one is a valid measurement and it says the same thing. The probe is
bound, enabled and refreshed at night and the water does not use it. I am not shipping a sky reflection that
is not there and I have not faked one with an emissive patch.

ONE ABLATION TOOK, ONE DID NOT — and the one that did not is a real finding

    albedo black and gloss gone:  water 89.90 against 92.51  (-2.60 of 255)
    moon light extinguished:      water 92.51 against 92.51  (+0.00)
                                  lane  33.57 against 33.57  (+0.00)

The albedo ablation is real: **the water's own albedo and gloss account for 2.60 of its 92.51 — three percent
of what you see.** Everything else in those pixels is a term the material does not own.

The moon-light ablation is VOID, and I can name why: `DuskSky.cs:380` writes `_moonLight.intensity =
moonUp * (0.30 + 0.22 * n) * (1 - occlusion * 0.92)` on every frame, so a harness that sets the intensity to
zero has it overwritten before the next capture — the harness's own read-back of "the moon light at 0.12" is
DuskSky's value, not mine. Reported as void rather than as physics. The harness cannot ablate that light
without pausing DuskSky, and that is the first thing the next attempt has to fix.

WHAT THE FRAME SHOWS, AND IT MOVES THE DEFECT

`m32c-water-mask.png` paints the puddles magenta, and the paint lands as ONE LARGE QUAD over the NEAR LANE —
not four small puddles. That is the composition problem in one picture: the decal's screen footprint is
mostly its damp halo lying over the lane, and the pixels it owns are the near-lane pixels.

Put that beside the numbers and the picture changes: the near ground (under the decal) is 92.51, the lane
beyond the decal is 33.57, and the field is 22.33. **The near lane is nearly three times the far lane.** The
puddle is not bright because it is water; the ground beneath and around it is bright because of where it is —
close to the camera at a shallow viewing angle. That is also exactly what Todd called "the lane glows like a
pale ribbon": the lane is far brighter in the near field than in the distance, and the puddle sits on top of
that and inherits it through its own transparency.

So the remaining suspect is the same for both halves of this milestone: **whatever makes ground brightness
climb steeply as the view flattens.** It is not albedo (pass 1 measured a 10 % albedo cut at 0.5 % of frame),
not the probe (0.14 of 255 on the water), not the water's gloss (2.60 of 92.51), and not settled by the
moon-light ablation (void). I am naming it instead of guessing at it, because the next pass can test it with
the same six-capture harness: measure the LANE's own brightness at near, middle and far, with the lane's
normal map unbound and with its smoothness zeroed, and one of those will move it.

ONE HONEST SENTENCE

**Does the puddle read as water? No.** It reads as a translucent pale patch lying on a pale near lane — the
water's own material accounts for three percent of what is visible there, and the sky is not in it. What I
would need to finish: DuskSky paused so the ablations are real, the mask narrowed to the water core (the core
is 7.6 % of the quad, so the halo is currently doing most of the painting), and the near-lane grazing
brightness identified — because that brightness is what both the lane and the puddle are actually wearing.

Verdict: M32c is OPEN. No done marker written. Pass 3 of the order's 4 used.
