WHAT I BUILT

A sky reflection probe, because there was none anywhere in the project — `ReflectionProbes.cs`, created by
the world builder after the ground and the puddles exist. Realtime, refreshed through scripting (the sky
barely moves, so one capture is enough), 128 px, box-projected, 130 x 114 m over the maze, refreshed from
the harness once the sky has actually reached night. Environment reflections are now set explicitly ON for
every ground material (`_ENVIRONMENTREFLECTIONS_OFF` cleared, `_EnvironmentReflections` 1, specular
highlights 1) rather than assumed — wired is not wired, and the material read-back confirms the values.

I also added one test hook, `FarmWalkerController.SetEyeHeightForTest`, so the acceptance frames can be shot
with the eye at 1.15 m as the order asks, and the harness's drift check now measures against the eye the game
is actually using rather than a fixed 1.655 m.

THE ACCEPTANCE PAIR — SAME CAMERA, ONE VARIABLE

    frame                        water mean G   lane mean G   water/lane   band mean   peak
    m32c-water-probe-on.png         91.68          29.02         3.16x        95.01     200
    m32c-water-probe-off.png        91.78          28.97         3.17x        95.21     200

Both shot standing low on maze cell (1,1) with the eye at 1.15 m, the puddle 3.93 m ahead, aimed 22 m down
the lane at a near-level pitch — the flattest view available, which is the angle a Fresnel reflection needs.
Probe present, enabled and refreshed:

    M32c reflection probe: present, realtime/scripted via scripting, enabled=True, box 130 x 114 m,
    refreshed now that the sky is at night

**And switching it off changes the water by 0.10 of 255.** The probe is in the scene and bound to the
materials, and it contributes nothing measurable — the A/B was taken with the probe disabled *and*
`_ENVIRONMENTREFLECTIONS_OFF` set on the water, and the frame did not move. So the water does **not**
currently carry a reflection of the sky, and I am not going to claim it does. Per the order, I have not
faked it with an emissive patch on the water.

What that means: the water's pale blue brightness comes from something other than the environment map. Two
candidates, both measurable next pass: the moon directional light's specular on the near-flat water normal,
and the broad lift that binding the M32 maps produces (the flagged 33.68 -> 49.45 whole-frame rise that the
M32 report could not explain). Until one of them is identified, anything I do to the water is guesswork.

THE PUDDLES

Four per level instead of six, 4.4 x 1.8 m instead of 3.4 x 1.7, long side along the lane. In
`m32c-water-probe-on.png` they read as pale blue-grey elongated patches lying in the lane's rut — no longer
the near-circular painted spots of the last pass.

I also found and fixed a real bug in the last pass's alignment test: it summed both of a straight-run cell's
neighbours, which point in exactly opposite directions and cancel, so it reported |dot| 0.00 for every puddle
in the maze while claiming to measure alignment with the moon's azimuth. It now takes one neighbour.

THE LANE, AND A FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE TINT

The order asked for lane vs field within 15 %, or the lane darker. Measured, same frame, lane and field
classified by the maze's own answer for the cell under each pixel (the water excluded):

    m32c-water-probe-on.png        lane 31.49   field 27.14   1.16x
    m32b-puddle-moon.png           lane 47.98   field 54.04   0.89x

So in the mirror-angle frame the lane is already darker than the field, and in the shallow frame it is 16 %
brighter — one point over the ceiling, not the 2-3x the order describes.

I scaled the lane's albedo tint by 0.90 to close that gap, and the material read-back proves the change
reached the shader:

    lane _BaseColor RGBA(0.846, 0.828, 0.792, 1.000)

**And the frame barely moved: the lane's mean went 48.22 -> 47.98, half a percent, for a ten percent albedo
cut.** That is the interesting result of this pass. The lane reading as a pale ribbon is *not* its albedo, so
it cannot be tuned away by tinting, and the next pass should measure what actually drives it rather than
turning that knob harder. I have left the 0.90 scale in place because it is verified, harmless and in the
right direction, and I have not chased it further on a guess.

ONE HONEST SENTENCE

Does the puddle read as water in the frame? **Partly, and not yet.** It reads as a flat pale patch lying in
the rut with a bluish cast, and at a glancing angle it measures 3.16x the lane — the water is unmistakably
the brightest surface there, which is the Fresnel behaviour the order described. But it does not carry a
visible reflection of the sky or the moon, and the probe that was supposed to put one there changes the frame
by 0.1 of 255. What it needs is the true source of its brightness identified, not another pass of tuning.

Verdict: M32c is OPEN. No done marker written.
