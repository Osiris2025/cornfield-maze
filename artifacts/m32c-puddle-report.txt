WHAT WAS FIXED, AND WHAT FOUND IT

Two changes, both in the material path:

1. **`PathMudWetness` was walking the lane past the matte rule.** `GameBootstrap` installs it, and on every
   frame it rewrote the lane material's `_BaseColor` **and its `_Smoothness`**, lerping smoothness toward 0.42 as
   the storm ran. The standing rule is that the ground is matte and any highlight on the lane is a defect; the
   M32 read-back measured 0.137 because it reads the *map's* alpha, not the constant this script overwrites.
   Two different numbers for one surface, and no read-back of the map could have caught it. Its smoothness is now
   capped by `PathMudWetness.MatteSmoothCeiling` (0.20) — wet dirt darkens, it does not grow a highlight.

2. **`Materials.MakeMatte`** — the lane and the field get no specular lobe and no environment reflection at all,
   structurally, rather than a low smoothness value a later edit could quietly raise. `GroundPuddle()` does not
   pass through it, so the water keeps its own gloss.

That clamp is what moved the picture. Measured on the masks, same frozen pose, same harness:

    state                    water region mean G   lane mean   lane/field
    before the clamp                92.51            33.55       1.50x
    after  the clamp                46.56            25.67       1.15x

**The lane is now at the order's ceiling — 1.15x the field, measured at the same distance — and the water
region lost half its brightness.** And the control proves the fix is real rather than a change of lighting: with
the lobe and the environment reflection put back on the lane, the same pixels read 32.46 against 25.67 (+6.79).
The highlight is real and the fix removes it.

THE ACCEPTANCE, LINE BY LINE

**A shallow-angle night frame, probe on and off, same camera.** Provided — `m32c-water-on.png` /
`m32c-water-off.png`, one frozen pose, every frame line pose-stamped. **But the probe contributes +0.00 of 255 on
the water** (mean |change| 0.07; water pixels brighter than 140: 0 with it and 0 without). The probe is in the
scene, enabled, bound and refreshed at night, and the water does not use it. The control says why: a lane at
mirror smoothness with environment reflections switched on, on top of that probe, does not brighten either —
**the probe's capture holds no sky.** So the frame does **not** show the water carrying a reflected sky, I am
not claiming it does, and no emissive patch was faked onto the water to hide it.

**The water's bright-pixel count and mean against the lane's, from the frame, not by eye.**

    water  54,405 px   mean G 46.56 of 255   bright(>140) 0 (0.0 %)
    lane    1,204 px   mean G 25.67          ->  water/lane 1.81x

**Lane vs field, same distance, same frame:** lane 25.67 against field 22.33 = **1.15x** — at the ceiling the
order set, not over it. (The lane mask shrank from 10,901 px to 1,204 px once the lane got darker, because a
dark lane painted cyan changes fewer pixels past the threshold; the sample is smaller and I am saying so.)

**The Fresnel direction is finally right.** On the water's own pixels the near half of the puddle means 40.76
and the far half — the grazing end, toward the horizon — means 52.37: brighter toward the horizon, falling off
as the view steepens, which is what a dielectric surface does. In the previous pass those two numbers were
identical to the decimal, because the thing being measured was the lane's false highlight rather than water.

**One honest sentence: does the puddle read as water in that frame? Yes.** In `m32c-water-on.png` the lane is a
dark wet gravel path — not a pale ribbon — and the puddles sit in the rut as pale, cool patches, brighter
toward their far end, unmistakably standing water rather than painted spots. What they are not is mirror-like:
there is no glint and no sky in them, because the probe delivers nothing and the moon light is too dim to make a
lobe on a flat surface. The sheen is a diffuse cool lift, not a specular sparkle. If Todd wants glass, that is a
different mechanism from the one this order proposed, and I would say so before building it.

WHAT I WOULD NEED, AND THE LESSON THAT COST FOUR PASSES

- **The probe question.** Its capture holds no sky. Either the sky dome is not in it (a realtime probe renders
  the scene, not the camera's skybox, and the dome is drawn by its own renderer) or the ground's environment
  path is inert. Reading the probe's cubemap back — the same trick that caught `alphaSource=None` — is the next
  single test, and it is cheap.
- **Two per-frame writers silently undid harness state changes:** `DuskSky.cs:380` rewrites the moon light's
  intensity every frame, and `PathMudWetness.Apply` rewrites the lane's colour and smoothness every frame.
  Ablations on either were VOID, and both were reported as void rather than as physics. **A runtime state change
  is not evidence until the thing that owns that state has been stopped** — the fourth form of the same defect
  in this milestone, after `alphaSource=None`, a copied harness that wrote the wrong report, and a paired A/B
  taken from two different cameras.

PASS-3 AND EARLIER, KEPT AS VOID

Pass 3's 92.51 / 33.57 / 1.50x were real *measurements* of the scene at the time, but the surface they were
reading was the lane's false highlight, not water: that is now fixed and those figures are superseded by the
table above. Passes 1 and 2 produced water-vs-lane numbers from two different cameras and then from a geometry
classifier that ignored the water's own material — both void, both labelled void rather than dropped, as
required.

Verdict: **M32c is NOT closed, and no done marker is written.** The outcome Todd asked for is met and measured —
the lane no longer glows (1.15x, at the ceiling) and the puddle reads as water in the frame — but one acceptance
line as written, "the water carrying a reflected sky", is not met: the probe ships nothing, and the order's
fallback clause covers a probe that ships sky-only, not one that ships nothing. That is Ernie's and Todd's call
to make with the frames in hand, not mine to certify.
