I have to lead with the bad news: last pass's headline was wrong.

THE INSTRUMENT WAS LYING — FOUND, PROVEN, FIXED

Every paired number this harness has produced, including the acceptance pair committed last pass, was taken
from TWO DIFFERENT CAMERAS. `Sample()` re-aimed the player but never re-established its POSITION, and the
walker keeps walking between samples, so "same camera, one variable" was metres apart. The drift figure
printed on every frame said 0.00 m either way, because it compares the camera to the player and never the
player to the stand — the same class of false green this milestone already hit once with `alphaSource=None`.

Proof, and it is not a matter of opinion — `scripts/m32c_frame_delta.py` (new) diffs two frames:

    probe-on vs probe-off, BEFORE the fix:  mean abs diff 9.658 of 255, 27.94 % of pixels differing by >8
    probe-on vs probe-off, AFTER  the fix:  mean abs diff 0.276 of 255,  0.01 % of pixels differing by >8

No 4.4 x 1.8 m decal can move 28 % of a frame. The fix: `Time.timeScale = 0` (the walker integrates on
deltaTime), the stand re-asserted twice around the re-aim, and every frame line now carries its camera POSE,
so two frames whose poses differ can no longer be quoted as a comparison.

THE PROBE ROUTE IS DEAD — a valid negative, from a frozen camera

Probe ON against probe OFF: mean abs diff 0.276 of 255, 0.01 % of pixels >8, band mean 95.59 vs 95.60, peak
202 vs 202. The reflection probe is in the scene, enabled, bound and refreshed at night, and it changes the
picture by a quarter of one level out of 255. **The water does not carry the sky by this route and I am not
going to ship that as done.** No emissive patch was faked onto the water, as the order requires.

THE ABLATION — and why it demolishes four passes of measurement

Four states, one variable each, same frozen camera (identical pixel sets and poses, so these compare):

    state                                   water region mean G    lane mean G
    shipped (probe on)                            92.01                30.69
    water _SpecularHighlights OFF                 92.44                36.40
    water gloss map unbound, _Smoothness 0        89.83                36.40
    water _BaseColor BLACK                        92.42                36.40
    MoonLight intensity 0                         92.44                36.40

The "water" region sits at ~92 of 255 with the water's albedo black, its gloss and specular removed and the
moon light extinguished. **No lit surface behaves that way.** Pixels that ignore every term the material owns
are not the material's pixels: the geometry classifier — corners in pass 4, camera rays in pass 1 — was never
measuring the puddle. That is why five passes in a row produced numbers nobody could reconcile, and it means
the water-vs-lane figures in every earlier report of this milestone are void. I have left them void rather
than quietly dropping them.

THE FIX FOR THE MEASUREMENT: ASK THE RENDERER, NOT THE GEOMETRY

`WaterFootprint()`: paint the decal with an unmistakable marker while keeping its coverage alpha, shoot the
same frozen camera again, and take the pixels that changed. That set IS the decal — no ray, no projection, no
degenerate rect. First honest numbers from it:

    WATER FOOTPRINT (marker method): water 36,208 sampled px of the frame, mean G 74.50 of 255,
    bright(>140) 1,466 (4.0 %). Same frame, same freeze: lane mean 42.43 over 18,450 px,
    field mean 22.11 over 5,176 px -> water/lane 1.76x, lane/field 1.92x

One caveat I am not hiding: the marker also flattens the normal map, so this set includes the damp halo
around the water core, not only the water. Narrowing the marker to an albedo-only change is the first job of
the next pass; the water core is 7.6 % of the quad by the baked maps, so the core's own mean will be lower
than 74.50.

LANE vs FIELD — both frames, frozen

    shallow frame        lane 42.43   field 22.11   1.92x   (field sample only 5,176 px)
    mirror-angle frame   lane 50.67   field 74.22   0.68x

The order's ceiling is 1.15x. In the mirror-angle frame the lane is comfortably darker than the field; in the
shallow frame it is 1.9x brighter, with a thin field sample dominated by the dark far ground. The lane is not
the 2-3x pale ribbon it was, but it is still the brightest thing on the ground in the shallow view, and I am
not calling it fixed.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE SCENE SINCE LAST PASS

Four puddles per level instead of six, 4.4 x 1.8 m instead of 3.4 x 1.7, long side along the lane. And a real
bug fixed in the old alignment test: it summed a straight-run cell's two opposite neighbours, which cancel
exactly, so it reported |dot| 0.00 for every puddle while claiming to measure alignment with the moon's
azimuth. It now takes one neighbour. The lane's albedo tint is scaled 0.90 (read back in the material as
`_BaseColor RGBA(0.846, 0.828, 0.792, 1.000)`), kept because it is verified, harmless and in the right
direction — measured on the fixed instrument it is worth half a percent, which is the honest reason not to
expect much from tinting.

ONE HONEST SENTENCE

**Does the puddle read as water? Partly — and better than the last pass.** In `m32c-water-probe-on.png` the
puddles are pale blue-grey patches lying in the lane's rut with a wet, glossy look, unmistakably water rather
than painted spots, and they measure 1.76x the lane. What they do not have is any reflection of the sky or the
moon: the moon is high in frame and there is no disc and no sky in the water, and the probe meant to put one
there moves the frame by 0.28 of 255. The frame also shows something new I do not like — a fine blue speckle
across the lane surface around the puddles that reads as glitter noise, not water. What I would need: the
marker narrowed to the water core, and the true source of the water's brightness found.

Verdict: M32c is OPEN. No done marker written. What is left is the milestone itself, not more instruments —
the next pass has a measurement that can be trusted, which the last five did not.
